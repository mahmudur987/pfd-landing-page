1. Introduction: The Spatial Gap

           

The health risks of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) are well-documented. In response, governments rely on air quality forecasts to issue public health warnings and formulate mitigation strategies. The quality of these forecasts directly impacts public health outcomes.

           

However, the majority of current forecasting models share a critical limitation: they treat monitoring stations or cities as independent, isolated time series. This approach is fundamentally misaligned with the physical reality of pollution.

           
                This misalignment is what we term the "Spatial Gap." It is a failure to acknowledge the first law of geography: "everything is related to everything else, but near things are more related than distant things."            
           

Pollution is not static; it is generated in one area and transported by wind to another. A model that ignores this interconnectedness cannot learn the true, generalizable physics of pollution dispersion. It can only "memorize" the local patterns of the city it was trained on.

3.2. Driver Analysis: From Correlation to Causation

           

The benchmark's SHAP analysis (your shap_summary.png) correctly identified TrafficIndex as a top driver. This tells us traffic is associated with pollution, but not by how much.

           

Our Causal ML analysis moves beyond this to estimate the true, unconfounded effect. We modeled the system using a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) to isolate the relationship we wanted to measure.

                       
           

After controlling for confounders (like Weather and Day of Week), we formally estimated the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) for the research question: "What is the average causal impact on PM2_5_next_day of a hypothetical 10% reduction in the TrafficIndex?"

                       
               

Causal Finding

               

ATE = -2.31 µg/m³

               

A 10% reduction in the TrafficIndex causes an average reduction in next-day PM2.5 of 2.31 µg/m³.
